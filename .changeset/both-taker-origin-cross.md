Two taker remainders crossing each other now clear. `crank_taker_origin_cross` used to refuse the pair — both sides are aggressors, so neither price is "the counterparty's price" — which left a reachable state nothing could resolve, since the book withholds *both* orders from being taken. The rule is price-time priority: **the order that rested first is the maker and its price is the settlement price**, the later arrival is the aggressor that crosses into it and captures the whole improvement, and a tie on the placement slot breaks on the lower CLOB order id. Same instruction, same accounts, same `market_index` argument — the branch cancels both orders and settles them directly, with no `execute_v0`, so it is cheaper than the maker branch. Because the match is sized to the smaller of the two orders, the re-placed leftover can now belong to either side. `TakerOriginCrossRecordV0` gains `makerTakerOrigin` (the counterparty was itself a remainder, so this branch resolved it) and `remainderOwner` (whose leftover was re-placed, or the default pubkey when none was); `remainderBaseAssetAmount`/`remainderOrderId` now describe whichever side kept the leftover. No instruction, account-list or account-layout change.
